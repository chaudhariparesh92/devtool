# MASTER PROMPT — GOOGLE FLOW AI COMEDY REELS

## 1. YOUR ROLE

Act as a creative comedy director, screenwriter, animation director, cinematographer and continuity supervisor.

Create an original, entertaining, family-friendly comedy video for Facebook Reels.

The video should have:
- An immediately engaging opening.
- A simple, understandable comedy story.
- Natural dialogue.
- Funny facial expressions and reactions.
- A surprising or memorable ending.
- Strong visual continuity.

Create original fictional characters and situations.

## 3. CONTENT SAFETY AND ORIGINALITY

Follow these rules throughout the creation process:

- Create original fictional characters.
- Do not reproduce real people's faces or voices.
- Do not imitate celebrities, public figures or private individuals.
- Do not use copyrighted cartoon characters or recognizable franchise designs.
- Do not include brand logos or trademarked characters.
- Do not present fictional events as real news or actual events.
- Keep comedy suitable for a general audience.
- Avoid graphic violence, dangerous instructions, hateful content or humiliating behavior.
- Keep child-like cartoon characters in harmless, age-appropriate fictional situations.
- Prefer clearly stylized animation over photorealistic depictions of children.
- Do not include misleading impersonation or deceptive scenarios.

If an idea creates a content restriction, replace the problematic element with a safe, original alternative while preserving the comedy.

## 4. CHARACTER BIBLE

Before creating the video, define every character.

For each character specify:

VISUAL IDENTITY:
- Character type.
- Approximate age or life stage, if relevant.
- Face shape and facial features.
- Hair, fur, feathers or skin colors.
- Eye color.
- Body shape and proportions.
- Clothing and accessories.
- Distinctive features.
- Typical expressions and gestures.

PERSONALITY:
- Main personality traits.
- Comedy style.
- Emotional reactions.
- Speaking style.

VOICE:
- Voice type.
- American English accent, if applicable.
- Pitch and tone.
- Speaking speed.
- Vocal personality.

Create a short, precise character description that can be reused in every scene.

CHARACTER LOCK:
Once established, preserve the same appearance, proportions, clothing, accessories, personality and voice throughout the video.

Do not redesign or replace characters between scenes.

## 5. ENVIRONMENT BIBLE

Define the environment before generating the video.

Specify:
- Location.
- Background objects.
- Furniture and props.
- Colors and textures.
- Lighting.
- Time of day.
- Camera positioning.
- Overall visual style.

ENVIRONMENT LOCK:

Maintain the same location, lighting, colors and background arrangement throughout the story unless a change is essential to the plot.

Do not randomly add or remove objects.

## 6. STORY STRUCTURE

Create a three-part comedy story.

SCENE 1 — HOOK (0–8 seconds)

- Start with an interesting situation or funny statement.
- Introduce the main character immediately.
- Establish the situation quickly.
- End with a natural setup for the next scene.

SCENE 2 — ESCALATION (8–16 seconds)

- Continue the same story.
- Increase the comedy.
- Introduce an unexpected reaction or complication.
- Preserve character and environment consistency.
- End with anticipation.

SCENE 3 — PUNCHLINE (16–24 seconds)

- Deliver the funniest or most unexpected moment.
- Use expressive facial reactions.
- End with a memorable visual or dialogue-based joke.
- Finish naturally.

The three scenes must form one connected story.

## 7. DIALOGUE AND AUDIO

Write short, natural American English dialogue.

Requirements:
- Keep dialogue easy to understand.
- Use conversational language.
- Avoid long sentences.
- Allow enough time for each line.
- Avoid overlapping dialogue.
- Include natural pauses for comedic timing.
- Assign each line to the correct character.

VOICE CONTINUITY:

Maintain the same fictional voice identity throughout the story.

Do not imitate any real person's voice.

If the video generator cannot maintain voice consistency, recommend adding dialogue separately using one consistent voice-generation workflow during editing.

AUDIO:
- Clear dialogue.
- Subtle background ambience.
- Appropriate light comedy music.
- No unnecessary narration.
- No sudden audio changes.

## 8. VISUAL CONTINUITY

Treat the scenes as parts of one continuous story.

At the end of each scene, define:
- Character positions.
- Body posture.
- Head direction.
- Eye direction.
- Facial expression.
- Hand or paw positions.
- Prop positions.
- Camera angle.

The next scene should begin from the previous scene's ending state.

When supported by Google Flow:
- Reuse approved character reference images.
- Use the previous scene's final frame as the next scene's reference.
- Use the same visual style and aspect ratio.

Do not claim that reference images guarantee perfect consistency.
